How to study a reaction

1. Identify C=C and count substituents.
2. Read the reagents.
3. Follow charge, radical, or bridge formation.
4. Rotate to inspect the approach face.
5. Predict connectivity and stereochemistry.
6. Compare the original slide and reveal the answer.

Model conventions

Models prioritise bond connectivity and approach faces. Grouped substituents are not single atoms. Ring conformations follow conformational-analysis rules: saturated six-membered rings are shown in chair form, five-membered rings are puckered/envelope-like, cycloalkenes retain planar sp² alkene carbons with a puckered remainder, and acyclic sp³ C–C bonds are displayed in staggered conformations. Transition geometries remain illustrative rather than energy-minimised. Inter-stage motion is schematic; read the discrete stages for chemical species.
