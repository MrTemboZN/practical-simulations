Method library & instrument anatomy

Understand every step

Select a metal and atomizer in the right panel to update the procedure, standards and 3D accessory.

This is a generic teaching instrument, not a manufacturer's digital twin. Signals, concentrations, timing and training acceptance limits are simulated. Real gas pressures, ignition sequences, lamp ratings, furnace programs and chemical preparations require the actual instrument manual, a validated SOP and supervised training.

    Atomic absorption, not emission

    Free ground-state atoms absorb narrow, element-specific radiation. The lamp supplies the analytical light; the atomizer converts the sample into atoms. Excited atoms and ions do not contribute to the same neutral-atom population.

    A = log₁₀(I₀ / I) = −log₁₀(T)
    A = mC + b   →   C = (A − b) / m

    The linear relation is valid only over the verified calibration range. AAS usually determines one element at a time. It does not by itself distinguish oxidation states, such as Cr(III) and Cr(VI), or identify molecular compounds.

    What happens in the atomizer?

    Flame: aspiration → aerosol formation → fine-droplet selection → desolvation → vaporization → dissociation → free atoms. Most aspirated liquid goes to the drain. Furnace: a discrete microlitre aliquot undergoes drying, pyrolysis, atomization and cleanout. Vapour techniques chemically separate the analyte before the absorption cell.

    Background correction

    A deuterium continuum source estimates broad background in suitable UV regions. Zeeman correction separates background using a magnetic field, often in furnace systems. Neither correction removes all line overlap, chemical suppression or contamination. The teaching signal shows the effect of disabling correction.

    Interferences and remedies

    Physical: viscosity changes aerosol delivery—match matrices or dilute. Chemical: stable compounds inhibit free atoms—optimize atomization or a validated modifier. Ionization: neutral atoms are depleted—use a validated ionization buffer. Spectral/background: use appropriate correction or another analytical line. Memory: rinse and check blanks.

    Standard additions

    When proportional matrix suppression persists, use equal sample aliquots with increasing known additions and the same final volume. Fit A against added concentration; the magnitude of the negative x-intercept estimates sample concentration in that final volume. Apply volume corrections. Standard additions do not remove additive background. This simulator uses external calibration; the matrix challenge demonstrates why a different calibration strategy may be required.

      Traceability & quality control

      Analytical batch notebook

      Preserve raw replicates, calibration, dilution factors, preparation details and quality-control decisions.

      Training criteria: calibration R² ≥ 0.995 and each non-zero level 90–110% recovery; ICV / CCV 90–110%; spike 80–120%; duplicate RPD ≤ 10%; blank ≤ 5% of lowest standard. These are simulator criteria, not universal regulatory limits. A high R² alone is insufficient. Sample precision must also be ≤ 5% RSD.

      Batch release checklist

      Run a closing CCV and blank after the unknowns. In this exercise a continuing check is also due after ten sample-related readings. Failed checks require investigation, recalibration and reanalysis of affected samples.

      Detection and quantitation

      Estimate detection from replicate low-level measurements and method-specific statistics. A simple 3s/m estimate is an introductory concept, not a validated MDL. Establish an LOQ with acceptable recovery and precision. Below the lowest non-zero calibration level, this simulator reports below the teaching quantitation range rather than an exact concentration.

      Activity log

      Shutdown and maintenance

      1. Rinse the introduction system until the signal returns to the blank baseline; measure a closing blank.
      2. Use the shutdown control. The simulator stops atomization, gas delivery and lamp power. Real flame systems require their manufacturer's controlled gas sequence; never improvise a sequence.
      3. Allow hot components to cool. Clean the burner and nebulizer or inspect the graphite tube and contacts as applicable. Check drain condition and segregate metal-containing waste.
      4. Save the report and investigate any unresolved QC flags before reporting analytical results.
