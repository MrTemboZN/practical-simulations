How to study an aromatic reaction

1. Decide whether the ring is aromatic (cyclic, planar, fully conjugated, 4n + 2 π electrons) and whether it is π-excessive (pyrrole, furan, indole, phenoxide, anilines, azulene 5-ring) or π-deficient (pyridine, quinoline, nitroarenes, azulene 7-ring).
2. Identify the reagent: an electrophile (NO₂⁺, Br₂/FeBr₃, acylium, ArN₂⁺, :CCl₂, CO₂, chloroiminium), a nucleophile (RO⁻, NH₂⁻, amines), a strong base (NaNH₂ → benzyne) or a leaving group that can depart on its own (N₂ → aryl cation).
3. For electrophilic substitution, draw the σ complex (arenium ion) for each possible position and count the resonance forms that keep an intact benzene ring or put + on a heteroatom with a full octet; the most stable σ complex gives the product.
4. For nucleophilic substitution, decide between addition–elimination (Meisenheimer anion: EWG ortho/para), elimination–addition (benzyne: very strong base, ortho H) and Sₙ1Ar (ArN₂⁺).
5. Follow every lone pair, π bond and charge; check that the last step restores the aromatic sextet.
6. Rotate the model: electrophiles and nucleophiles attack the π face of the ring (perpendicular to the ring plane); the σ complex carbon becomes sp³ with E and H above and below the plane.

Model conventions

Aromatic rings are drawn as one Kekulé structure; the real ring has equal (delocalised) bonds, and the charge of a σ complex or Meisenheimer anion is spread over several ring atoms — the model shows one resonance form, as in the lecture notes. Carbon-bound H atoms are implicit except where they take part (the H lost in substitution). Grouped labels (CH₃, OCH₃, Ph …) are single centres. sp carbons and N are linear (180°), sp² trigonal planar (120°), sp³ tetrahedral (109.5°). Metal complexes (ferrocene, Cu, Al) are schematic. Inter-stage motion is schematic; read the discrete stages for the chemical species.
