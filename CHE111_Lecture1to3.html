Standard Form, Significant Figures and Measurements

Undergraduate chemistry foundations

Report every measured quantity with a numerical value, an appropriate unit, and precision justified by the measuring instrument.

Standard form

Write a non-zero number as a × 10n, where 1 ≤ |a| < 10 and n is an integer.

0.000450 = 4.50 × 10−4

Significant figures

All non-zero digits count. Zeros between significant digits count. Leading zeros do not count. Trailing zeros count only when the decimal notation makes them explicit.

0.004050 has 4 s.f.

Measurement statement

Record a measured value and its uncertainty to the same decimal place. Include the unit outside the brackets.

V = (25.00 ± 0.03) mL

Calculation and reporting rules

Apply the rule required by the mathematical operation, then round once at the end.

Addition and subtraction follow decimal places; multiplication and division follow significant figures.

Repeated-measurement summary

Enter replicate values separated by commas or spaces. The uncertainty is estimated as half the range for this introductory treatment.

The mean, range, half-range, relative uncertainty and sample standard deviation will appear here.
SituationReporting ruleExample
Analogue scaleEstimate one digit beyond the smallest marked division when the scale permits.A 1 mL graduated cylinder scale may justify an estimated reading such as 18.4 mL.
Digital instrumentRecord every displayed digit unless the instrument is unstable or specified otherwise.A balance displaying 2.347 g is recorded as 2.347 g.
Addition or subtractionRound to the least number of decimal places among the inputs.12.11 + 0.3 = 12.4
Multiplication or divisionRound to the fewest significant figures among the inputs.4.56 × 1.4 = 6.4
Exact counts and defined factorsDo not limit significant figures.3 samples and 1000 mL = 1 L are exact.
Core measurement relationships absolute uncertainty = ±(maximum − minimum)/2 relative uncertainty (%) = (absolute uncertainty / mean) × 100
Accuracy describes closeness to an accepted value. Precision describes agreement among repeated measurements. A precise data set can still be inaccurate if systematic error is present.
